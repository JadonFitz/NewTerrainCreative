New Terrain Creative Getting started
Onboarding

Everything we need, in one place.

Three things get us from signed to shooting: access to your ad account, half an hour of thinking before our first call, and a folder of brand assets. None of it takes long, and doing it this week is what keeps the first campaign live by week two.

01

Access, not ownership

You keep everything. Your ad account, your Facebook Page, your Instagram, your tracking data, and the card the media budget is billed to all stay in your name. We are added as a partner with permission to work inside them.

We will never ask for your password, and you should never give one to an agency.

Granting access, three steps

  1. Create a business portfolio, if you don't have one Go to business.facebook.com and set one up in your business name. If you already run ads, you almost certainly have one.
  2. Add New Terrain Creative as a partner Business settings → Partners → Add partner
    Our business portfolio ID 1393983659459788
  3. Assign the assets we need Your ad account, your Page, and your dataset or pixel. Meta will ask what level of access to give for each. We need to be able to create and manage, not just view. Business settings → Accounts → Ad accounts → Assign partner

What this lets us do

  • Create, run and optimise your campaigns
  • See performance and spend
  • Install and read conversion tracking
  • Build audiences from your own data

What it does not let us do

  • Post or comment as you
  • Message your customers
  • See your personal Facebook profile
  • Move money or change your payment method

To remove us, open Business settings, find New Terrain Creative under Partners, and click remove. It takes one click and needs no conversation with us. We would rather you know where that button is on day one than wonder about it later.

02

Before the offer call

Week one is a conversation about what we are actually asking people to say yes to. Everything downstream is built on it, so it is worth thirty minutes of thinking beforehand. You do not need to write anything up. Just arrive having considered these.

What you sell

  • What is the one thing you would most like to sell more of
  • What does a customer pay, on average, the first time
  • What is a customer worth over the whole relationship
  • How many more could you genuinely handle a month before something breaks

Who says no, and why

  • The three objections you hear most, in the words people actually use
  • What makes someone pick a competitor over you
  • What do people misunderstand about what you do

Who says yes

  • Describe your single best customer. Not the biggest, the best
  • What did they want when they first called you
  • Where do your good customers tend to come from today

What you have tried

  • What has worked, even a little, and what you think made it work
  • What has failed, and what you spent finding out
  • Anything you are running right now that we should not disrupt

Bring real numbers if you have them, even rough ones. A guess you can defend is more useful than a blank, and it is far better to be corrected by the data in week seven than to have never had a number at all.

03

What to send us

Send whatever you have to business@newterraincreative.com and we will set up a shared folder. Missing items are not a problem, they just tell us what we need to make.

Brand

  • Logo files, vector if you have them, with any variations
  • Brand colours and fonts, or a brand guide if one exists
  • Anything you consider off limits visually

Existing material

  • Any footage worth cutting into ads, however rough
  • Product or premises photography
  • Reviews and testimonials, including screenshots
  • Ads you have run before, especially ones that worked

Practical

  • Your website login or a way to add tracking, if we are connecting to your existing site
  • Where new leads should arrive: an inbox, a CRM, a phone
  • Who on your team approves creative, and how fast they can turn it around

Shoot day

  • We shoot on location at your business, usually a single day
  • Who is on camera. Owners outperform actors in this format, consistently
  • Wear what you would wear to meet a good customer. Avoid tight patterns and logos that date
  • Anyone who appears on camera signs a short release on the day

One thing worth deciding early: the approval step in week two is the most common place a launch slips. If one named person can approve creative within forty eight hours, the campaign goes live on schedule. If it needs a committee, tell us now and we will build the timeline around it.
