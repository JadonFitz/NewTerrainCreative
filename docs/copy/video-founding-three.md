## Rules this script is written under

- The words "VSL" and "funnel" never appear. Not in the video, not on the
  page around it.
- No guaranteed leads, revenue or return. Not softened, not implied.
- The waived month is bounded out loud. "Free" without edges is the claim
  we are trying not to make.
- Ad spend is named as the client's, on the client's card, in the video.
  If someone is surprised by it later, this script failed.
- A founding partner is not described as a paying client, and their
  results are not described as a case study we already have.
- No Apple Maps. It came out of the site and it stays out of here.
  `scripts/vsl-growth-engine-v2.md` still contains it and is stale.
- No b-roll direction. Direction is for delivery only.

## OPEN · 0:00 → 0:20

If you run a business that people come to in person, and your marketing
still isn't turning into customers, this is for you.

You're paying somebody to make content. You're paying somebody else to
run ads. Leads are landing somewhere. And none of it is connected.

*[Flat. This is a statement of fact, not a hook. Do not sell yet.]*

## THE PROBLEM · 0:20 → 1:02

Most businesses don't have one marketing problem. They have a
disconnected one.

The content gets made. The ads get launched. Somebody fills out your form
at 8:47 on a Tuesday night, and nobody sees it until Wednesday morning.
By Wednesday morning they've already called somebody else.

And because the pieces are separate, nobody can tell you which part
worked. So you can't fix it. You can only spend more.

So we start somewhere else. Not with what content should we make. With
what are we trying to get a customer to do. Then we build backwards.

## WHAT WE ACTUALLY DO · 1:02 → 1:44

Offer. Creative. Distribution. Conversion.

First we get the offer clear: who it's for, what it solves, what you want
them to do next.

Then we build the creative around that offer. Not content in general.
Creative with a job.

Then we put money behind it, so it reaches the right market instead of
waiting on an algorithm to feel generous.

Then we make sure the lead has somewhere to land and reaches your team
while the person is still interested. Not the next morning.

## WHY IT MATTERS · 1:44 → 2:04

A good video on its own isn't a marketing strategy. Neither is running
ads.

An ad can't fix a weak offer. Good media buying can't fix bad creative.
And nothing fixes a lead nobody called back.

That's why they're one thing here instead of three vendors.

*[End of shared core. The retainer cut branches here.]*

## THE OFFER · 2:04 → 3:10

Here's the part I'd want to hear if I were you.

We're taking on three businesses, and for the first thirty days we're
not charging a service fee.

Not because it's a discount. Because we're building this practice around
four kinds of business — health and wellness, law, dental, and
construction and contracting — and we would rather prove it on three real
ones than talk about it.

**Here's how the first month works.** One strategy session. One
production day at your place. One batch of campaign creative, cut and
graded. One round of revisions on it. We build and launch the campaign,
we manage it for the rest of the thirty days, and you get a written
report at the end.

That's the whole month. It's a real month of work and it has edges.
Anything past it is quoted like normal work, because it is.

## THE PART PEOPLE LEAVE OUT · 3:10 → 3:48

Two things nobody says in a video like this, so I'm going to.

The first is ad spend. You fund your own. At least fifteen hundred
dollars over the month, billed by Meta to your card on your ad account.
It never comes to us and we never touch it. Without it there's no
campaign, so it's the one thing that isn't optional.

The second is what happens after. If it works and you want to keep going,
that's thirty-five hundred a month. If you don't, the month ends and
that's genuinely the end of it. There's no clause that turns one into the
other. You'll see that number in writing before you send the application,
not after.

## WHAT I'M NOT PROMISING · 3:48 → 4:10

I'm not going to tell you what this will return. I don't know your
market, I haven't seen your numbers, and anybody who quotes you a figure
on a first campaign is guessing at you.

What I'll tell you is what we'll do, what it covers, what it costs, and
what we learned at the end of it. In writing.

## CLOSE · 4:10 → 4:35

Three places. The application takes about two minutes, and it's in two
parts: the first asks whether this is even right for you, and the second
shows you the terms before you send anything.

If it's not a fit, we'll say so on that page rather than book a call to
tell you the same thing thirty minutes in.

*[No urgency in the read. The scarcity is real — three is three — so
saying it once plainly lands harder than pushing it.]*
