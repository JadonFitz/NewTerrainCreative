Production Media,
end to end.

This is the document we leave behind after a conversation. It sets out how we work, what each engagement includes, what it excludes, and what it costs, so you can weigh it without us in the room.

It is not a proposal. A proposal names your benchmark, your production allocation and your term. This names the shape of the thing.

02

Most businesses don't have one marketing problem. They have a disconnected one.

The content gets made by one person. The ads get run by another. The leads land somewhere nobody is watching. Somebody fills out a form at 8:47 on a Tuesday night and hears back on Thursday, by which time they have already called a competitor.

And because the pieces are separate, nobody can tell you which part failed. So there is nothing to fix. There is only more to spend.

03

Offer, creative, distribution, conversion.

Offer

Who it is for, what it solves, what you want them to do next. Everything downstream is built on this and most campaigns skip it.

Creative

Built around that offer. Not content in general. Creative with a job, cut for the placement it will run in.

Distribution

Paid media so it reaches the market you chose, rather than waiting on an algorithm to hand it to you.

Conversion

Somewhere for the lead to land, and a path to your team while the person is still interested.

    06

    The 90-Day Production Promise.

    If, after 90 consecutive days, the campaign has not improved the primary performance benchmark we agree upon during onboarding, the next standard production cycle is on us.

    One benchmark, chosen with you and written into the scope before launch, with its baseline and its measurement source recorded at the same time. It has to be measurable: qualified cost per lead, qualified-lead rate, landing-page conversion rate, click-through rate, or another measure we both agree on. Total views does not qualify by default.

    It applies where the agreed ad spend was maintained, tracking stayed operational, the campaign ran the full period, and the offer, funnel and targeting were not changed underneath the measurement. The remedy is one standard production cycle matching your contracted monthly allocation, up to a value named in the agreement, with one revision round. It excludes ad spend, management fees, software, talent, locations, travel, permits, props and third-party costs. It is a service credit, not a cash refund, and it cannot be combined with a waived first month.

    Where it applies: Growth Partner and Brand Builder. Not The Anchor, and not The Anchor with the campaign-management add-on. The promise turns on us having managed the campaign and been able to change the creative in response to what it showed. On production-only work we are doing neither, so a claim could never succeed, and a promise that cannot pay out is worse than no promise. It is one of the things the step up to Growth Partner buys.

    This is a promise about our work, not about your revenue. We are not guaranteeing leads, sales, ROAS or any other business result. Full terms are provided before anything is signed.

    Internal: full conditions are drafted in docs/terms/90-day-production-promise.md and are not attorney-approved. Six questions are still open on them, including whether the promise can attach to a production-only engagement at all.

    07

    Always separate.

    • Advertising spend. Paid by you, directly to the platform, on your own account and payment method. Never marked up, never held by us.
    • Paid placements and media purchases.
    • Talent, and licensing a specific commercial track. Music from our licensed libraries is included.
    • Locations, permits and props.
    • Travel outside Los Angeles or Tampa.
    • Third-party software and subscriptions.

    Campaign work assumes a working lead path: a page, a form and tracking that fires. Where that does not exist yet, building it is the first piece of work rather than an assumption.

    08

    How a quarter actually runs.

    Month one

    Offer and strategy, production, campaign build, launch. The benchmark and its baseline are agreed and documented before anything goes live.

    Month two

    The first honest read. What the creative did, what the spend bought, where the lead path leaks. Usually the month with the most uncomfortable conversation in it.

    Month three

    Acting on what month two showed. New creative against what worked, and the first quarter where the numbers mean something.

    This is why the minimum is three months rather than a preference. Two months is a shoot and a shrug: the data has not accumulated enough to tell either of us anything, and we would rather turn the work down than sell you a quarter that cannot answer its own question.

    09

    What you see.

    Where paid is running, admin access to the ad account is part of the engagement, because we do not report on numbers we cannot see. You keep ownership of the account, the pixel and the data throughout, and you keep them if we part ways.

    Reporting cadence and depth vary by package and are named in the proposal.
